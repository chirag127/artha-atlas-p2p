# Taxation of Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Lending in India

## 1. Classification of Income
Under the **Income Tax Act, 1961**, returns earned from P2P lending by individual retail lenders are classified under:
- **Head of Income**: *Income from Other Sources (IFOS)* governed by **Section 56(2)**.
- **Tax Rate**: Added to total gross taxable income and taxed at the **applicable marginal slab rates** (ranging up to 30% plus applicable surcharge and 4% Health & Education Cess).
- **No Preferential Capital Gains Treatment**: Interest received on peer loans does not qualify for preferential capital gains rates (such as Section 112A or 111A).

---

## 2. The Asymmetric Tax Trap: Bad Debt Non-Deductibility

The most dangerous pitfall for Indian retail P2P lenders is the statutory asymmetry between taxable interest and defaulted principal:

### Statutory Provisions
- **Section 36(1)(vii)** permits bad debts to be written off as a deduction **ONLY** for assessee taxpayers carrying on a business or profession (*Profits and Gains of Business or Profession - PGBP*), such as licensed NBFCs or banks.
- **Section 57** specifies allowable deductions under *Income from Other Sources*. Section 57(iii) permits deductions for expenses laid out wholly and exclusively for earning such income (e.g., annual platform subscription fees or recovery agent commission fees).
- **Crucial Limitation**: Section 57 **contains NO provision** allowing an individual to deduct unrecovered principal losses or defaulted loans against interest income!

### Practical Example of the Asymmetric Trap
Suppose an investor deploys ₹1,00,000 across 20 borrowers:
1. **Borrowers 1-18** pay ₹14,000 in gross interest.
2. **Borrowers 19-20** default entirely, resulting in ₹10,000 lost principal.
3. **Net Cash Outcome**: The investor's actual economic gain is only ₹4,000 (₹14,000 interest minus ₹10,000 capital loss).
4. **Income Tax Liability (30% Bracket)**:
   - Taxable IFOS Income: ₹14,000 (full interest).
   - Allowable Bad Debt Deduction: ₹0.
   - Tax Due (at 31.2% effective rate): ₹4,368.
5. **Net After-Tax Outcome**:
   $$\text{Realised Gain} = \text{Cash Gain (₹4,000)} - \text{Tax Paid (₹4,368)} = \mathbf{-₹368}$$
   **The investor experienced a real financial loss while paying substantial income tax to the government.**

## 3. Platform Fee Deductions under Section 57(iii)

Platform origination, servicing, or withdrawal fees charged by NBFC-P2Ps can be legally deducted under Section 57(iii) provided:
1. The fee was incurred *wholly and exclusively* for the purpose of generating the taxable interest.
2. The platform provides a formal GST invoice or periodic fee statement.
3. The claim is supported in the taxpayer's audit trail during assessment.

---

## 4. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) under Section 194A

- **Applicability to Individual Borrowers**: Section 194A mandates TDS on interest payments made by banking companies, cooperative societies, and corporate entities. Individual borrowers taking personal loans are generally **exempt from deducting TDS** unless their business turnover exceeded ₹1 Crore in the preceding financial year.
- **Corporate & MSME Borrowers**: If a P2P loan is issued to a private limited company or MSME borrower, TDS of 10% under Section 194A is deducted if annual interest exceeds ₹5,000.
- **Reconciliation with Form 26AS & AIS**: Lenders must ensure TDS certificates (Form 16A) from corporate borrowers are reconciled against their Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS.
- **Tax Liability Remains Slab-Based**: Even if no TDS was deducted at source, the lender is legally required to declare full gross interest and pay advance tax under Section 208/209/210.

---

## 5. Tax Planning Best Practices for P2P Investors
1. **Quarterly Advance Tax Compliance**: Calculate accrued P2P interest quarterly (June 15, Sept 15, Dec 15, March 15) to prevent Section 234B/234C penal interest charges.
2. **Platform Statement Archival**: Download annual interest ledgers, recovery logs, and fee invoices before platform financial year closure.
3. **PGBP Assessment for Professional High-Net-Worth Portfolios**: For investors deploying high capital (above ₹20L) who maintain active loan selection, legal consultation regarding filing under PGBP with formal books of accounts may be evaluated, acknowledging the accompanying audit obligations under Section 44AB.
