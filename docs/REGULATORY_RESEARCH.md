# Indian NBFC-P2P Regulatory Research & Framework Analysis

## 1. Statutory Mandate & Authority
Peer-to-Peer (P2P) lending platforms in India are regulated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) under the powers conferred by:
- **Section 45-IA**, **Section 45-IB**, and **Section 45-IC** of the **Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934**.
- Notification No. DNBR.045/CGM (CDS)-2017 dated August 24, 2017, classifying NBFC-P2Ps as Non-Banking Financial Companies.
- **Master Direction – Non-Banking Financial Company – Peer to Peer Lending Platform (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2017** (Updated as of August 16, 2024 via Circular `RBI/2024-25/63 DOR.FIN.REC.35/03.10.001/2024-25`).

---

## 2. Permitted Activities vs. Express Prohibitions

### Permitted Scope
An NBFC-P2P is strictly an **intermediary and marketplace facilitator**. It is permitted to:
1. Act as an online platform bringing prospective borrowers and lenders together.
2. Carry out credit assessment and credit bureau score pull for prospective borrowers.
3. Facilitate execution of tripartite or quadripartite loan contracts.
4. Assist in collection and recovery of loans originated through the platform.
5. Provide secure escrow infrastructure with a scheduled commercial bank trustee.

### Statutory Prohibitions
An NBFC-P2P **CANNOT**:
1. **Accept deposits** under Section 45I(bb) of the RBI Act.
2. **Lend on its own balance sheet** or take balance-sheet credit exposure.
3. **Provide First Loss Default Guarantees (FLDG)**, credit insurance, credit enhancement, or capital protection guarantees in any form.
4. **Offer assured, minimum, or fixed returns** (e.g. "Earn 12% guaranteed like a Fixed Deposit").
5. **Permit international fund flows**; lenders and borrowers must be Indian residents with verified domestic bank accounts.
6. **Cross-sell insurance or third-party products** with linked credit terms without clear unbundled consent.

## 3. The Landmark August 16, 2024 Regulatory Crackdown

In early 2024, supervisory inspections revealed widespread regulatory arbitrage: several platforms pooled funds into unsegregated escrow accounts, offered "instant liquidity" repurchase arrangements, matched lenders automatically without counterparty transparency, and disguised credit guarantees through affiliate balance sheets.

On **August 16, 2024**, the RBI issued comprehensive remedial directions establishing stringent guardrails:

### Key Directives of the August 16, 2024 Review
1. **T+1 Fund Deployment Timeline**:
   - Platform escrow accounts cannot hold idle investor cash indefinitely.
   - Once a lender deposits funds into the escrow account, the funds must be disbursed to the matched borrower by **T+1** (the next business day), or refunded to the lender's bank account.
2. **Prohibition of "Auto-Invest Liquidity Pools"**:
   - Platforms cannot run pooled withdrawal features where existing lenders are paid out by assigning their loans to incoming new lenders before tenure completion.
   - Any early exit can only occur via bilateral assignment where the incoming lender explicitly reviews and selects the specific underlying loan contracts.
3. **Mandatory Direct Counterparty Disclosures**:
   - Lenders must be presented with full borrower profiles (credit bureau grade, income bracket, monthly debt obligations, loan purpose) **prior to loan allocation**.
4. **Independent Escrow Trustee Mandate**:
   - Escrow accounts must be operated exclusively under the mandate of a Trustee registered with SEBI and maintained with a Scheduled Commercial Bank.

## 4. Statutory Prudential Limits

| Dimension | Statutory Limit | Regulatory Section |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Max Aggregate Lending Across All P2P Platforms** | ₹50,00,000 (Fifty Lakhs) | Master Direction Section 6(1) |
| **Net Worth Certificate Requirement** | Required for investments > ₹10,00,000 | Certified by a Practicing Chartered Accountant |
| **Max Exposure to a Single Borrower** | ₹50,000 across all loans | Master Direction Section 6(2) |
| **Max Aggregate Borrowing Across All Platforms** | ₹10,00,000 (Ten Lakhs) | Master Direction Section 6(3) |
| **Maximum Loan Tenure** | 36 Months | Master Direction Section 6(4) |

## 5. Fair Practices Code & Recovery Guidelines
NBFC-P2Ps are bound by the RBI's **Fair Practices Code (FPC)**:
- **No Harassment in Debt Collections**: Prohibits calling borrowers between 7:00 PM and 7:00 AM, inappropriate language, contacting family members or friends, or public shaming.
- **Mandatory Board-Approved Grievance Redressal Policy**: Escalation matrix with Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) contact details published on website.
- **RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme**: Lenders and borrowers have direct access to the RBI Ombudsman if complaints are unresolved after 30 days.
